Messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines deliver a synthetic transcript encoding a pathogen antigen, most commonly a viral surface protein, into host cells. The mRNA is encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) that protect it from degradation and facilitate cellular uptake. Once inside the cytoplasm, host ribosomes translate the mRNA into the antigen, which is then presented on major histocompatibility complex molecules, priming both antibody and T-cell responses. Nucleoside modifications, such as the substitution of uridine with N1-methylpseudouridine, reduce innate immune activation and increase protein expression. Because mRNA does not enter the nucleus and is not integrated into the genome, the platform carries no risk of insertional mutagenesis. The approach enables rapid vaccine design, since only the antigen sequence must change between targets, and was central to the swift development of COVID-19 vaccines.
